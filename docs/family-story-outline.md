# A family in the records — editorial proposal

**For Andy's review · 22 September 2026 · proposal only**

## The story to tell

**Working title:** _A Family in the Records_
**Central question:** How can I tell the story of the family I came from when some relationships are directly documented, others emerge only through several records together, and Benjamin's parents remain unknown?

**Thesis:** I began with a search for my biological line. The records carry it from Benjamin and Hannah in Kentucky through Missouri and Texas to me, with different strengths of evidence at each handoff. They also reveal a wider family through wills, land, households, work, and possible naming memory. Knowing that network does not resolve every parent-child link. My Meason history adds to the Cohen family that raised me.

**Opening image:** Joseph Meason, dying aboard a boat near Pittsburgh in 1799, asks for a deed that cannot be reached among the packed effects. Witnesses preserve his words. Fourteen years later, scattered claimants—including Benjamin—convey interests in Highland Creek. This opens the wider family story without establishing a direct ancestral line.

## Factual boundaries for the draft

- Thomas and Joseph belong to Benjamin's **collateral kinship investigation**; neither is established as his direct ancestor. The 1813 deeds do not name his father or exact sibling branch.
- Joseph's requested deed was inaccessible; its execution, recovery, and legal effect are unknown. His spoken declaration alone did not meet Kentucky's written-land-devise requirement. The later deeds do not prove relatives thwarted him.
- George M.'s placement with Benjamin is **high-confidence indirect**, not a direct parent-child statement. Describe later handoffs by their own evidence strength and omit private documents and unsupported modern locations.
- Co-residence, proximity, work, and a recurring surname support a Missouri network, without settling all Hollingsworth relationships, a Kipper work arrangement, or a name's intended honoree.
- Use citations and provider links, not privately archived provider images or an unreviewed “complete transcription.”
